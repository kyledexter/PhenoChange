<section class="page-hero"><div class="shell narrow"><p class="eyebrow">About PhenoChange</p><h1>A network for monitoring change in the seasonal tropics.</h1><p class="lead">PhenoChange brings together researchers using ground-based cameras to understand vegetative phenology in tropical dry forests and savannas.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="shell content-grid"><aside class="side-note"><p>Vegetative phenology</p><span>The timing of leaf production, canopy development, senescence and leaf loss.</span></aside><div class="prose"><h2>The challenge</h2><p>The ecosystem function of tropical dry forests and savannas depends critically on vegetative phenology. These seasonal patterns regulate carbon exchange, water and energy fluxes, nutrient cycling and productivity.</p><p>Climate change is altering rainfall, temperature and the frequency of extreme events, but the consequences for the timing of leaf production and loss remain poorly resolved across much of the seasonally dry tropics.</p><h2>What the network does</h2><p>PhenoChange uses ground-based camera systems—often called phenocams—to create high-frequency records of vegetation greenness and canopy condition. The network will support comparable monitoring across sites and provide a platform for collaborative research.</p><h2>Initial objectives</h2><ul><li>Connect camera-based phenology monitoring across tropical dry forests and savannas.</li><li>Develop and share consistent approaches for camera deployment, image processing and analysis.</li><li>Quantify variation in phenology among ecosystems, vegetation components and regions.</li><li>Determine how climate variability and global change are reshaping seasonal vegetation dynamics.</li><li>Strengthen links between near-surface observations, field ecology and Earth observation.</li></ul><div class="notice"><strong>This page is ready to expand.</strong> Add the project's formal aims, geographic scope, work packages and network history as these are finalised.</div></div></div></section>
